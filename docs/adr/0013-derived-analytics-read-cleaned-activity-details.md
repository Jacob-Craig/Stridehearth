# 0013. Derived Analytics Read Cleaned Activity Details, Computed on Demand

Status: accepted.

Stored Activity Details stay exactly as the device recorded them. Derived analytics (Grade-Adjusted Pace and the metrics built on it) do not read them directly: they call Telemetry Cleaning, a pure function from Activity Details and sport to Cleaned Activity Details, every time they compute. Cleaned Activity Details are never stored and never shown in place of Activity Details. When the cleaning rules or an analytic's formula change, the derived data is recomputed from the stored details, the way Best Efforts are (ADR-0003); the Parser Version rises only when decoding the same activity file would produce different Activity Details or Laps (issues #26, #31).

Telemetry Cleaning rejects GPS spikes (a speed above a sport-keyed ceiling, or a distance step that implies one, goes backwards or takes no time), treats heart rate as missing when it reads 0, steps more than 30 bpm per second or tracks step rate within 3 bpm for 60 s or more while following it (cadence-lock), interpolates speed and heart-rate gaps whose good neighbours are at most 10 s apart, and averages altitude over a centred 15 m window of cleaned distance. A distance spike removes only its own excess: the bad step is replaced by the distance the cleaned speed covers and later samples shift by the difference, so samples outside a spike keep their recorded step lengths.

## Considered Options

- **Clean during ingestion and store the result as the Activity Details**: Rejected. It destroys what the device recorded, so a better rule could never be applied to Activities already imported without their files, and every rule change would have to raise the Parser Version and Upgrade every Activity.
- **Store Cleaned Activity Details beside the Activity Details**: Rejected. It doubles the largest table for series that cost a few linear passes to rebuild, and adds a second copy to keep in step on every Upgrade, Backfill and sport edit.
- **Let each analytic do its own filtering**: Rejected. Grade-Adjusted Pace, Training Load, peaks and interval detection would each pick different thresholds and disagree about the same Activity.
- **Compare each distance sample with the last good one**: Rejected. A teleport offsets every later sample, so this would discard the rest of the Activity instead of one step.

## Consequences

- Any stored derived data must record which version of its algorithm produced it, so a change to Telemetry Cleaning can trigger a recompute; that version is separate from the Parser Version.
- Best Efforts and Personal Records keep scanning the recorded distance. Moving them to Cleaned Activity Details would silently change existing Personal Records, so it needs its own decision.
- An Activity with no GPS fix keeps its speed, distance and altitude as recorded; only the heart-rate rules apply to it.
- The speed ceilings are 8.5 m/s for a Run, 5 m/s for a Walk or Hike, 30 m/s for a Ride and 3 m/s for a Swim. A Workout has none, because it is the fallback for unrecognised sports.
- Stored cadence is strides per minute of one leg, so cadence-lock compares heart rate with twice the sum of the cadence and its fractional part (2 × (cadence + fractionalCadence)), and only for a Run, Walk or Hike.
- Cadence-lock needs heart rate to follow cadence, not merely to sit at step rate (#52). A stretch within 3 bpm of step rate for 60 s or more is a lock only if, at either end, the median heart rate over the 20 s just outside it differs by 10 bpm or more from the median over the 20 s just inside it (the sensor locking on or letting go, abruptly or over a short ramp; medians keep a brief glitch from counting), or step rate varies over the stretch (standard deviation of at least 1 step/min) and heart rate correlates with it at 0.6 or more, or the stretch both starts and ends the heart rate recorded. An end that cannot be judged for another reason (a recording pause, a dropout, too little heart rate in the window) is left to the other end. A true heart rate that drifts into and out of a steady step rate, as on a tempo run or race at about 170, is kept, and with it Efficiency Factor, Aerobic Decoupling and the heart-rate inputs of Training Load and the VO2max Estimate.
- Accepted risk: a lock that slides into a steady step rate more slowly than 10 bpm in 20 s and stays while cadence holds within about 1 step/min is indistinguishable from a coincidence and is kept, so its heart rate reads as step rate; so is a lock with one end at a recording pause or the edge of the recording and the other unjudgeable. A true rise of 10 bpm in 20 s that ends at step rate (the start of a hard repetition) and then stays within 3 bpm of a steady step rate for 60 s is taken for a lock. A recording whose heart rate is at step rate from its first sample to its last is discarded, as before.
- Grade-Adjusted Pace reads grade over a long baseline, not between nearby samples (#51). A FIT barometer records altitude in 0.2 m steps and altitude from GPS (a watch without a barometer, a phone) wanders by a metre or two, and the 15 m altitude window averages neither away: measured over ±10 m, one barometer step read as about a 1% grade, so flat-ground GAP moved several percent either side of pace from sample to sample (up to about ±10% when the reading flickered between two steps). Because Minetti's cost is convex (C(i)/C(0) = 1 + 5.42·i + 12.86·i² + …), zero-mean grade noise with standard deviation σg also raises mean GAP by about 12.9·σg², and NGP, a fourth-power mean, by more: 1–2 m of GPS altitude noise put flat-ground mean GAP 1.5–9% and NGP 1.7–12% above pace. Grade is therefore the slope between two anchors, the mean distance and altitude over 60 m of cleaned distance centred 90 m behind and 90 m ahead of the sample (sliding inwards whole near the ends of the Activity). An anchor window with no sample, across a gap in the recorded distance, takes the nearest sample outwards from the sample being graded, so a gap never brings the anchors closer together than the baseline minus one window. #32's "a flat run's GAP is within 1% of its pace" is read, as the owner's triage of #51 decided, as within 1% over every 5 s rolling window, because Peak Efforts, D′bal and interval detection read GAP sample by sample (#38, #40, #47). In an Activity at least 240 m long, a 0.2 m step now reads as 0.11% grade at an even pace (GAP 0.6% above pace) and at most 0.2 / 120 = 0.17% (GAP 0.9%) when uneven pace, such as standing still near an anchor, pulls the anchors' mean distances together. In a shorter Activity of length L the anchor window is min(60 m, L/2) wide and the baseline is L minus that width (L − 60 m from 120 to 240 m, L/2 below 120 m), so the step reads proportionally steeper (0.2% grade at an even pace over a 160 m Activity, whose baseline is 100 m). In an Activity at least 240 m long, 1–2 m of GPS altitude noise (lag-one correlation 0.9) leaves mean GAP and NGP within 1% of pace (tested on a 3600-sample, 10.8 km run); in a shorter one the shorter baseline reads the same noise as a steeper grade shared by many samples, so GAP and NGP can be several percent off pace (below 120 m every sample gets the same grade).
- Accepted risk: the 180 m grade baseline costs resolution on short climbs. A 200 m climb at 5% reads close to Minetti's ratio (within 0.02 of 1.30) only at its middle and less towards its ends, while GAP rises over the 120 m of flat either side of it, so a climb's GAP is spread over a longer stretch at a lower peak; climbs much shorter than the baseline read well below their grade. Very noisy GPS altitude still biases the flat upwards: about 1% for NGP at 2 m of noise with lag-one correlation 0.95, about 2.5% at 3 m. NGP and Efficiency Factor from such devices therefore remain only roughly comparable with barometric ones.
- Accepted risk: any single distance step faster than the speed ceiling counts as a GPS teleport. That includes a genuine catch-up step after a GPS outage, where the watch kept recording a frozen distance and then jumped once the fix returned, so the distance covered during the outage is lost from the cleaned distance and from anything derived from it, such as Grade-Adjusted Pace.
- Speed gaps longer than 10 s stay missing, so consumers must handle missing samples instead of assuming a continuous series.
